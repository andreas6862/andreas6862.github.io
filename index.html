🧭 What This App Is For

Team Fortress 2 rewards players who plan. The problem is that most planning ends up spread across browser tabs, phone notes, old screenshots and half-finished text files you can never find again. The Class Loadout Toolkit pulls all of that into a single desktop window built for Windows.

You create a profile for each class, attach the weapons you actually run, write short notes about the maps where that setup works, and then log what happened in your matches. Over a few weeks those logs turn into charts that show you where you are improving and where you keep falling short.

Everything is manual and player-driven. You decide what to record, you type the notes, and you review the numbers. The app simply gives all of it a home, with a clean interface and a look inspired by the 2026 Team Fortress 2 community tooling scene.

✨ What Is Inside

🗂 Class Profiles

Create one profile per class — Scout, Soldier, Pyro, Demoman, Heavy, Engineer, Medic, Sniper, Spy. Each profile holds your preferred role, your playstyle note, and a short reminder of what you are trying to fix in your next session. Switching between classes takes a single click, so you never lose your place mid-scrim.

🔫 Weapon Loadouts

Save multiple loadouts per class and name them clearly — "payload attack", "defense hold", "koth fast rotate". Add the primary, secondary and melee you use, plus a note on why that combination works. When the meta shifts, you have a record of what you were running before, so you can compare and adjust instead of guessing.

🗺 Map Notes

Attach notes to the maps you play most. Hold positions that work, routes you like, spots to avoid, and the loadout that performed well there. Notes are searchable, so when a map loads you can pull up your own advice in seconds.

🎯 Manual Aim Practice

A built-in practice planner lets you set a session length, pick a focus (tracking, flicking, crosshair placement), and record how the session felt. You build a routine over time and the app keeps the history so you can see whether your practice is actually consistent week to week.

📋 Match Tracking

Log each match with the class you played, the map, the result, and a one-line takeaway. It takes about fifteen seconds, but after twenty matches you have a real picture of which classes and maps you actually win on.

📈 Gameplay Analytics

The analytics view turns your match log into simple charts: win rate by class, win rate by map, streaks, and how your aim practice frequency lines up with results. No spreadsheets, no formulas to write.

⚙ Config and HUD Friendly

The toolkit keeps a tidy folder for tf2-cfg and tf2-hud related notes and snippets, so small tweaks you like are recorded in one place next to the loadouts they belong to.

🧩 Your First Ten Minutes

Start with the class you play most. Create the profile, add one loadout you trust, and write two sentences about how you want to use it. That is already more structure than most players have.

Next, add a map note for the map you play most often. Write down the one position that always works for you and the one you keep dying in. Future you will thank present you.

After your next session, log two or three matches. Do not log all of them — just the ones you remember clearly. Consistency matters far more than volume here.

Finally, open the analytics view after about a week. Even a handful of entries starts to show patterns, and those patterns are the whole point of the tool.

💡 Habits That Make It Useful

  • Name loadouts after situations, not after weapons. "Hold last point" is easier to find than "Loadout 3".
  • Write map notes right after a match, while you still remember what happened.
  • Keep aim practice sessions short and repeatable. Twenty focused minutes beat a two-hour session once a month.
  • Update a profile when your setup changes, instead of creating new ones endlessly.
  • Review the analytics view once a week, not after every match. Weekly review is where it pays off.

🛠 Common Questions

The file will not open

Check that your download finished completely and that you are double-clicking the file itself rather than a shortcut. If Windows blocks it, right-click the file, open Properties, and tick the unblock option at the bottom of the window.

Where is my data stored?

Profiles, loadouts, notes and match logs are saved locally on your own PC. Nothing is uploaded anywhere, and the app does not need an account or an internet connection once it is installed.

Can I back up my notes?

Yes. Copy the application's data folder to a USB stick or a cloud drive folder every so often. Restoring is simply copying it back to the same place.

Does it work on Mac or Linux?

The toolkit is built for Windows PCs. Other operating systems are not covered by the download provided above.

🔒 Privacy and Performance

All records live in local files on your machine. There is no telemetry, no login and no background service running while you play. The app sits idle in a window and uses very little memory, so it will not compete with Team Fortress 2 for resources.

Close it whenever you like. Your data is written to disk as you type, so nothing is lost if you shut your PC down straight after a session.
